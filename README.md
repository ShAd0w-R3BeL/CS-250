My CS 250 Journey: Reflecting on Software Development
How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
To interpret user needs, I start by listening and asking questions to understand what the user wants to achieve. This often means looking beyond a simple request to uncover the underlying goal. For the SNHU Travel application, a user might say, “I want to find a vacation spot,” but their true need could be to find a sunny, budget-friendly destination for their family trip.

This is where user stories become essential. They help bridge the gap between abstract user needs and concrete programming tasks. A user story, structured as “As a [type of user], I want [some goal] so that [some reason],” forces me to think from the user's perspective. For example, the story “As a traveler, I want to view travel options sorted by climate so that I can find a destination that fits my preferences” directly translated into the programming task of adding a climate-based filtering function. This process ensures that every feature I build has a clear purpose and directly addresses a user need.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
My approach to developing programs is iterative and collaborative. I believe in starting with a minimum viable product and then building on it through continuous cycles of feedback and refinement. The SNHU Travel project was a great example of this, as we focused on one feature at a time, ensuring it worked correctly before moving on to the next.

Looking ahead, I hope to incorporate several Agile processes into my future development work. Daily stand-ups are a key practice I'll use to maintain team alignment and quickly address any roadblocks. I also value sprint retrospectives because they provide a structured way to reflect on what went well and what could be improved, leading to continuous growth. Finally, the use of a visual product backlog, like the one we used in Trello, is crucial for maintaining transparency and keeping the entire team on the same page.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
Being a good team member in software development goes beyond just writing good code. It’s about communication, empathy, and adaptability. It means actively listening to others' ideas, providing constructive feedback, and being willing to help teammates when they are stuck. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned the importance of clear communication, especially when we had to adjust our scope to integrate a new API. By using pseudocode and holding open discussions, we were able to navigate a challenging situation together.

Ultimately, a good team member is someone who contributes to a positive team culture. They are reliable, transparent about their progress, and committed to the shared goal. The best teams are not just groups of individuals but cohesive units where every member supports the others, leading to a more efficient and enjoyable development process.
